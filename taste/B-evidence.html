What can stop your money

Three mechanics can hold or reverse a payment. None of them has a promised resolution time.

A complaint inside the hold window

Prizes are held for 48 hours before they can be spent, so other entrants can raise a complaint. A complaint raised in that window stops the prize until it is resolved, and the resolution can go against you.

A review of the account

A payment can be held while an account is reviewed. Identity checks are required before you can cash out, and again at higher amounts.

A payout name that does not match

Money leaves only to a bank account in the same legal name as the Boadman account. A mismatch stops the payout until the account details are corrected.

What it argues, and what it gives up

The argument is that a page which is honest when it has nothing to show is more persuasive than one that is confident when it does. So live state leads, and every absent, stale, zero and degraded reading is composed as a first-class state rather than caught as an error. The page can be read top to bottom with the counters gone, the board empty and the rates unreadable, and it still says what the product does, what it costs, and what you can lose.

What it sacrifices: comprehension of the full money path. This direction never draws the journey from card to bank; it shows the readings the machinery produces and trusts the reader to infer the machine. A visitor who wants a walkthrough gets the Terms and the FAQs, not a diagram. It also spends its first screen on a data panel rather than on art, which makes it the least immediately atmospheric of the three.

Type

Four families drop to three on this route. Space Grotesk keeps the display and money job because it has tabular figures and holds at 68px. Inter keeps the body job because it disappears, which is what body type should do on a page a sceptic is scanning. Barlow Condensed keeps the small uppercase state-label job. JetBrains Mono is dropped from this route: it only appeared in the review rig here, which uses the system monospace stack. That is one fewer font file on the page with the tightest LCP budget.

Where the brand line sits, and why

Compete with discipline. is safe alone and unsafe by adjacency, so it gets a full-bleed band of its own with at least 168px of clear space above and below it at desktop, its own surface, and only one other line for company: a plain description of how a competition is decided. Nothing regulatory, institutional or compliance flavoured is inside that field. It is deliberately not the h1: three directions all keep this line, so if it led all three heroes the heroes would be interchangeable and the divergence test would fail. Here the argument is carried by the headline and the line is given a room instead of a podium.

State rules proposed

  • Zeros are never printed. A quiet platform and a partial database failure return the same four zeros, so a zero at display size could be publishing a lie. If all four read zero the frame prints the written composition instead. If some read zero and others do not, the zero figures are replaced by a sentence, never the glyph.
  • The stats do not live in a removable strip. They live inside a permanent framed panel in the hero. The panel always renders; only its contents branch. The page therefore cannot reflow around a missing section and cannot develop a hole, which is a stronger answer than removing the section from the DOM.
  • Staleness threshold: ten minutes. Under ten minutes the panel prints the read time. Between ten and sixty minutes it prints that the reading is over ten minutes old and adds a warning line, with no figure attached to the age. Past sixty minutes the panel treats the reading as absent.
  • The hero chip is deleted, not justified. The top competition no longer renders twice. The hero carries the platform reading, the board carries the competitions, and nothing in the hero vanishes at 1280px.
  • Units win over the current labels. Pool, guarantee and entry render with an explicit unit and full separators, never compacted: 12,400 coins. The cell is budgeted for 1,250,000 coins; the rig proves it.
  • The Featured badge is removed. Rather than branch it on row count, the ordering is stated once in the section introduction. A badge that can be featured against nothing is a bug with a design cause.
  • Board templates. Three rows use a 1.25 / 1 / 1 grid at 1280 and above, a full-width lead card over a two-up below that, and a single column under 1024. Two rows use an even pair. One row keeps a 560px maximum and takes a written note beside it rather than stretching. Zero rows replace the grid with the empty composition.
  • Rates never appear bare. Each figure is set inside the sentence that qualifies it, so it cannot be lifted into a tile, a headline or a comparison. If the config cannot be read, the page prints none rather than a substituted default.

Auth, links and header

Signed in is the same page with swapped CTAs. No re-order, no truncation, no extra section. Only the header CTA, the hero CTA and the player panel CTA vary, and their labels differ in width so none of them is sized to one string. The greeting resolves display name, then username, then the literal fallback, which the rig demonstrates across the three authed viewers.

Anonymous markup contains only allowlisted destinations. Gated destinations such as /dashboard appear only in the signed-in variants, where they are not gated for that viewer. In production those anchors are rendered conditionally on the server and are simply absent from the anonymous document. This comp keeps all four variants in the DOM and hides three with CSS purely so the rig can switch viewers inside one file; nothing gated is reachable by pointer or keyboard in the anonymous state either way.

Competition cards link to /p/tournament/{id} in every auth state, because one anonymous-safe URL is simpler to reason about than a per-viewer branch and the detail page serves both.

The page is dark in every section and never inverts. There is no light variant, because the production token file does not define one, and inventing a second surface set would put this route and the compliance console on different foundations. The only theme swap in the system is [data-theme="compliance"], which is an admin surface and never reaches this page.

The header is sticky at 68px and translucent, so nothing load-bearing passes beneath it. There is no scroll-driven header behaviour. Below 1024px the nav becomes a real disclosure with aria-expanded, Escape to close and focus returned to the trigger, which the current page does not have at all.

Cookie banner

Restyled rather than treated as immovable. It still occupies roughly the bottom 200px at 1440x900 and 390x844, so no CTA or load-bearing proof is placed there: the hero footing bar sits well above it at both sizes. Manage preferences opens a real modal with a focus trap, Escape, and focus returned. The footer control opens the same modal, which gives the banner’s promise a destination it currently does not have.

Art

  • Card plates are procedural CSS, not images: layered radial and linear gradients, a repeating radial arc field, a scan striation, and a soft radial mask, with three variants seeded per card position. They are the art-free default the wire actually returns today, not a fallback, and they rebuild at any size. The rig can force all three identical, which is the likely shape if covers ever land, since the three cards sort by pool.
  • Hero, discipline and readings fields are marked with IMAGE-SLOT comments carrying complete standalone generation prompts. Until those are generated they render as layered CSS placeholders built from the same vocabulary as the card plates.
  • No third-party game art, logo, title, character or HUD element appears anywhere, including backgrounds, textures, alt text and metadata. One competition names Chess, which is unbranded.

Metadata and icons

  • <title>: Boadman - competitions, coins, and what they cost. 49 characters.
  • Description: 126 characters, carrying the cost, the loss and the age gate, and no regulatory, protection, earnings, geographic or SLA claim.
  • OG and Twitter card at 1200x630, slot og-card. The mark and the line are composited as real layers in production rather than baked into the generated field, so the locked mark is never redrawn or distorted and no figure is ever set into an image.
  • Favicon at 512, 192 and 32, and an apple-icon at 180: a straight crop of the helmet from the locked mark file on the page background, at viewBox="0 0 31.07 54.58". A crop, never a redraw.
